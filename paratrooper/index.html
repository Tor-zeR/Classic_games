Airborne — Game Guide & Strategy

Rating: ★★★★★ 4.5/5 (98 votes)

History & Origins

Airborne is a tribute to Paratrooper, a 1982 DOS game developed and published by MicroArts. Paratrooper was one of the defining titles of the early PC gaming era, distributed widely as shareware and installed on an enormous number of home IBM-compatible computers throughout the 1980s. The core concept — defending a fixed ground position from waves of airborne attackers — taps into the classic turret-defence fantasy with immediate, satisfying gameplay. Players rotate a ground-mounted anti-aircraft gun to track and destroy helicopters, biplanes, and paratroopers before the latter can land, form groups, and physically dismantle your turret. The escalating spawn rate and the creeping threat of accumulated paratroopers creates a compelling tension curve. Classic Arcade's Airborne extends the original concept with retro neon visuals, progressive difficulty waves, and a high-score chase that rewards both accuracy and target prioritisation.

Scoring System

Aircraft score points based on type: slower biplanes award fewer points than faster jets, and helicopters transporting paratroopers are the highest-value targets. Each paratrooper shot mid-air awards points; paratroopers that successfully land and begin walking toward your turret must be shot on the ground. You lose the game when paratroopers reach and capture your gun emplacement. Lives represent your turret health — each successful paratrooper assault reduces it.

Pro Tip

Rotate your turret past the target's current position slightly to lead the shot — aircraft move horizontally and your shells travel at a finite speed. Helicopters hovering directly above are the easiest shots; jets at shallow angles are the hardest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can paratroopers actually destroy my turret?

Yes. Paratroopers that land safely will walk toward your gun emplacement from both sides. If enough of them gather and surround the turret, they dismantle it — ending the game. You must prioritise clearing landed paratroopers even while aircraft continue to attack overhead.

Q: Is it better to shoot helicopters or their paratroopers?

Helicopters award more points and prevent multiple paratroopers from deploying, so targeting the aircraft before it releases its payload is generally the better strategy. However, if paratroopers are already landing near your position, clearing the ground threat takes priority.

Q: Does the difficulty increase over time?

Yes. Wave speed, aircraft quantity, and the rate of paratrooper deployment all increase as you survive longer. Later waves feature faster, smaller aircraft that are harder to track with the rotating turret.
